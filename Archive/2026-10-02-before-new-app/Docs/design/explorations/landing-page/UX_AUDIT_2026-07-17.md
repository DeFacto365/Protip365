# Landing-page UX audit

Date: 2026-07-17
Scope: the three original ProTip365 landing-page explorations
User goal: understand the product quickly and decide whether to get the Android app

## Evidence reviewed

1. `01-shift-ledger.png` — poor hierarchy. Three hero devices compete with a workflow row, a long capability inventory, privacy, pricing, and repeated calls to action.
2. `02-week-in-focus.png` — clearer schedule focus, but three screenshots and detailed workflow explanations make the page read like documentation.
3. `03-private-shiftbook.png` — strongest hero, but the story is repeated in two more dense sections. It also contains corporate logos, deprecated clock-in/out language, and unsupported PDF export copy.

## Highest-impact corrections

- Keep one promise in the hero: plan shifts across employers and compare expected with actual earnings.
- Use one primary Google Play action and show iOS quietly as `Coming soon`.
- Show one dominant, legible app screenshot rather than three simultaneous devices.
- Limit the explanatory content to three short outcome-led benefits.
- Reduce privacy and pricing to one compact reassurance section.
- Move the complete feature inventory to the store listing, product documentation, or help content.
- Use employer initials and color coding; do not use corporate logos.
- Say `user-reported actual start/end and breaks`, never live clock-in/out.
- Say `CSV export and encrypted backup`, never PDF export.

## Accessibility risks visible in the drafts

- Supporting text and in-product labels become too small at common viewport sizes.
- Some light gray copy may not provide sufficient contrast.
- Dense visual regions increase scanning and cognitive effort.
- Repeated calls to action compete rather than clarify priority.

Static images cannot verify semantic heading order, keyboard operation, focus visibility, screen-reader labels, responsive reflow, zoom behavior, or final color contrast. These must be tested in the implementation.
